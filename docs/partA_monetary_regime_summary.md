#Part A : Ukraine’s monetary regime, 2000–2025

This document summarizes the preliminary analysis of Ukraine’s exchange-rate and monetary regime.

#Main conclusion

Ukraine did not have constant monetary sovereignty throughout the sample.

During several periods, the National Bank of Ukraine maintained a de facto or official exchange-rate anchor, which limited independent monetary policy. Monetary sovereignty was strongest during episodes of exchange-rate adjustment, devaluation, and the inflation-targeting period after 2016.

This matters for the counterfactual because hypothetical Euro Area membership should not be modelled as a constant treatment. The treatment intensity is higher when Ukraine had genuine monetary autonomy and lower when monetary policy was already constrained by an exchange-rate peg or wartime controls.

#Key periods

| Period | Approximate UAH/USD regime | De facto regime | Key events | Monetary sovereignty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2000–2004 | Stabilised around the dollar | De facto peg / stabilised arrangement | Post-transition stabilisation | Low |
| 2005–2008 | Around 5 UAH/USD | De facto dollar peg | Pre-GFC stability | Low |
| 2008–2009 | Sharp depreciation | Managed adjustment / crisis regime | Global financial crisis | High during peg break |
| 2010–2013 | Re-stabilisation | De facto peg / stabilised arrangement | Post-GFC stabilisation | Low |
| 2014–2015 | Major depreciation | Crisis float / managed float | Crimea, Donbas, balance-of-payments crisis | High |
| 2016–2021 | Inflation targeting | Inflation-targeting regime with managed flexibility | NBU reform and IT adoption | Medium to high |
| 2022–2023 | Wartime fixed exchange rate and capital controls | Fixed / heavily managed wartime regime | Full-scale invasion | Low to medium |
| 2023–2025 | Managed flexibility | Managed float under wartime constraints | Gradual return to flexibility | Medium |

#Implication for Part B

The counterfactual model uses a time-varying treatment-intensity index.

The exchange-rate channel is removed or attenuated more strongly during periods in which Ukraine had genuine monetary sovereignty and less strongly during periods in which monetary policy was already constrained by a peg, capital controls, or wartime fixed exchange-rate arrangements.
